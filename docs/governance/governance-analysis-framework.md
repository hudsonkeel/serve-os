## Purpose

This document defines the standard analytical posture and finding hierarchy used whenever a governance module is compared against existing Serve organizational documentation (a "canonicalization" pass — see, for example, `docs/governance/workforce/background-eligibility/canonicalization-report.md`). It applies to every current and future governance module, not just Background Eligibility.

## Governing Principle

**Treat the current Serve Policies & Procedures — and other existing approved organizational documents — as the organization's baseline operating specification.**

Governance exists to inherit, organize, and formalize existing approved operational practice. It does not exist to second-guess that practice by default. A difference between a governance module and current policy is not, by itself, evidence of a problem — new governance modules are frequently *incomplete* relative to years of accumulated operational practice, not wrong.

Existing Serve operational documents are assumed to represent the organization's current approved operating position **unless there is specific evidence to the contrary.** The burden is on a finding to justify treating existing practice as suspect — not on existing practice to justify itself.

A governance/policy difference should only escalate beyond routine inheritance when one of two things is true:

1. **A deliberate leadership decision to change the existing practice**, or
2. **A verified conflict with applicable regulation** — not a hypothetical one, not a citation that merely looks incomplete.

Recommending legal review is not the default response to a governance/policy difference. It is reserved for the fourth category below, and only when there is actual evidence of inconsistency with a specific regulatory requirement — not simply because two documents happen to say different things.

## The Finding Hierarchy

Every difference found during a canonicalization pass must be classified into exactly one of these four categories.

### 1. Inherited Operational Standard

An existing, approved organizational practice that governance is simply missing and should adopt as-is. This is the default classification for any difference where current practice is operating, has no evidence of regulatory conflict, and governance simply hasn't caught up to it yet.

*Resolution path:* governance is extended to formally incorporate the existing practice. No legal review, no leadership decision beyond acknowledging the inheritance.

### 2. Governance Enhancement

A genuinely new capability, process, or improvement that has no current-practice counterpart, or that deliberately supersedes current practice with something better. This is a leadership decision — not because anything is legally uncertain, but because it's new and worth a deliberate yes.

*Resolution path:* leadership reviews and approves (or declines) the enhancement. Not a legal question unless the enhancement itself touches regulation.

### 3. Regulatory Reference Update

An editorial or citation-level improvement only — a miscited statute, an inconsistent reference style, a citation that should point to a more precise source. The underlying substance is not in question, only its citation or presentation.

*Resolution path:* correct the citation. Does not require the deeper legal review reserved for category 4.

### 4. Verified Regulatory Conflict

Existing organizational practice **appears** inconsistent with an applicable regulation, based on specific, identifiable evidence — not merely because governance and policy differ, and not merely because a citation looks incomplete. This is the only category that warrants recommending legal review, and the finding should state exactly what evidence produced the "appears inconsistent" conclusion.

*Resolution path:* legal review, specifically scoped to the identified inconsistency.

## How to Apply This

When performing a canonicalization pass:

1. Start from the assumption that current practice is correct and approved.
2. For each difference found, ask: *is this simply something governance hasn't captured yet?* If yes — Inherited Operational Standard. Stop there; do not escalate further.
3. If the difference is a genuinely new idea with no current-practice counterpart — Governance Enhancement.
4. If the only issue is how something is cited or referenced, not what it says — Regulatory Reference Update.
5. Only reach for Verified Regulatory Conflict when there is specific evidence — not general unease — that current practice may not satisfy a specific regulatory requirement.

## Origin

This framework was adopted after a review of the Background Eligibility canonicalization report found that five of nine findings had been defaulted to "requires legal review" without evidence of actual regulatory inconsistency — several of which were, on closer inspection, simply existing operational practice (an already-functioning appeal process, already-enforced registry checks) that governance had not yet caught up to. See the Background Eligibility Module's `decision-log.md` for the record of that revision.
