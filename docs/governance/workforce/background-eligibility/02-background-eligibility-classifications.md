# 02 — Background Eligibility Classifications

**Framework:** Serve Workforce Governance Framework
**Module:** 1 — Background Eligibility
**Version:** 0.1 (Draft)
**Status:** Draft — Pending Legal & Executive Review

**Last Updated:** 2026-07-08

---

This document defines each of the four Background Eligibility Classifications introduced in [`01-background-eligibility-ontology.md`](./01-background-eligibility-ontology.md). For the offenses that map into each classification, see [`06-offense-taxonomy.md`](./06-offense-taxonomy.md). For the process by which a classification is reached, see [`05-review-workflow.md`](./05-review-workflow.md).

---

## 1. Eligible

**Definition:** The background investigation revealed no findings that raise a reasonable concern for client safety, organizational trust, or the risk domains defined in [`03-risk-domains.md`](./03-risk-domains.md).

**Typical basis:** No criminal history; or findings limited to matters such as traffic citations, parking violations, expired registration, and isolated municipal violations that do not implicate any risk domain.

**What happens next:** The applicant proceeds through the remainder of the hiring process without any background-related restriction. No further background-specific review is required.

**What this classification is not:** Eligible does not mean "background check skipped" or "background check favorable on balance." It means the investigation was completed and produced no finding that requires further scrutiny under this framework.

---

## 2. Reviewable

**Definition:** The background investigation revealed findings that do not meet the criteria for Automatic or Presumptive Disqualification, but that require individualized review before a classification can be finalized.

**Typical basis:** Offenses such as simple possession, a single misdemeanor DWI, minor theft or shoplifting, disorderly conduct, trespassing, criminal mischief, or non-violent resisting arrest — where the organization's judgment is that context matters. See [`06-offense-taxonomy.md`](./06-offense-taxonomy.md) §3 for the full representative list.

**Review factors:** Individualized review under this classification considers, at minimum:

- The nature and severity of the offense.
- Time elapsed since the offense or conviction.
- Evidence of rehabilitation.
- Employment history since the offense.
- Relevance of the offense to the specific role or duties in question.
- Whether the finding reflects an isolated incident or a pattern of repeated behavior.

**What happens next:** The investigation proceeds through the individualized review procedure in [`05-review-workflow.md`](./05-review-workflow.md) §4. The output of that review is a final classification decision that is documented and retained — Reviewable is not itself a permanent end state; it resolves, with rationale, into a documented outcome.

**What this classification is not:** Reviewable is not a soft "probably fine" outcome and it is not a workflow status meaning "still being processed." It is a classification that specifically means: *this case requires a documented, individualized judgment before the organization can say more.*

---

## 3. Presumptive Disqualification

**Definition:** The background investigation revealed findings that presumptively disqualify the applicant, subject to executive-level review before that presumption is finalized.

**Typical basis:** Offenses such as felony theft, burglary, forgery, embezzlement, drug distribution, repeated domestic violence, or felony DWI. See [`06-offense-taxonomy.md`](./06-offense-taxonomy.md) §2 for the full representative list.

**Why executive review, and not automatic:** These findings are serious enough that disqualification is the organization's starting position, but not so categorically severe that no circumstance could ever warrant an exception (unlike Automatic Disqualification, §4 below). Executive review exists to confirm the presumption applies to the specific facts, and to document the reasoning either way.

**What happens next:** The case is escalated to the executive review tier defined in [`05-review-workflow.md`](./05-review-workflow.md) §5. The outcome of that review — whether it upholds the presumption or, in a documented exceptional circumstance, overrides it — is recorded as part of the audit trail for that applicant.

**What this classification is not:** Presumptive Disqualification is not a synonym for "will not be hired." It is a strong, documented presumption against eligibility that only executive-level review can finalize or override — and every override must be justified in writing and retained.

---

## 4. Automatic Disqualification

**Definition:** The background investigation revealed findings that categorically disqualify the applicant from employment, with no discretionary review available under this framework.

**Typical basis:** Offenses directly implicating violence, sexual misconduct, abuse or exploitation of vulnerable persons, or healthcare/financial trust violations against a client. See [`06-offense-taxonomy.md`](./06-offense-taxonomy.md) §1 for the full representative list.

**Why no discretion:** Given Serve Caregiving's population — elderly and vulnerable clients, often in their own homes, often without a third party present — some categories of finding present a risk the organization is not willing to accept under any circumstance. Removing discretion at this tier is itself a deliberate risk-management decision, not an oversight.

**What happens next:** The classification is final upon matching. No further individualized review, executive override, or appeal is available under this framework. (Whether any legal right to reconsideration exists independent of this framework — for example, under applicable fair-employment or ban-the-box law — is a question this framework flags as **Requires Legal Review** rather than resolves.)

**What this classification is not:** Automatic Disqualification is not a "strong recommendation to decline." It is the one classification in this framework designed to leave no room for individualized override, and that design choice is deliberate given the population Serve Caregiving serves.

## 5. Summary Table

| Classification | Discretion Available | Typical Trigger | Resolves Via |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eligible | N/A — no restriction | No qualifying findings | Immediate |
| Reviewable | Individualized review required | Lower-severity, context-dependent offenses | Standard review procedure |
| Presumptive Disqualification | Executive override only, documented | Serious offenses short of the Automatic tier | Executive review tier |
| Automatic Disqualification | None | Violence, sexual misconduct, abuse/exploitation of vulnerable persons, healthcare/financial trust violations | Immediate, final |
