## 1. Purpose

Serve Caregiving places caregivers into the homes and lives of some of the most vulnerable people in North Texas. That access is a privilege the organization extends only after a consistent, defensible evaluation of an applicant's background.

This module exists to answer one question, and answer it the same way every time it is asked:

> **Given everything Serve Caregiving lawfully knows about an applicant's criminal history and related background investigation findings, what is that applicant's Background Eligibility Classification?**

Everything in this module — the ontology, the four classifications, the risk domains, the workflow, the offense taxonomy — exists to make that single determination consistent, explainable, and auditable, regardless of who performs the review or when.

## 2. Scope

### 2.1 In Scope

This module governs the evaluation of criminal background investigation findings for:

- All applicants for employment with Serve Caregiving, regardless of role.
- All existing employees subject to re-screening (periodic, for-cause, or upon role change).
- Contractors, volunteers, and temporary staff who may have direct or indirect access to clients, client residences, protected health or financial information, or organizational assets.

### 2.2 Out of Scope

This module deliberately does **not** govern:

- **Hiring decisions.** A Background Eligibility Classification is one input to a hiring decision. It is never the entire decision. See [`01-background-eligibility-ontology.md`](./01-background-eligibility-ontology.md) §4 for the formal distinction.
- **Role eligibility or role assignment.** Whether a specific classification permits placement in a specific role is governed by the interaction of this module with the role's exposure profile (see [`04-role-exposure-model.md`](./04-role-exposure-model.md)), not by this module alone.
- **Skills, qualifications, references, or interview evaluation.** These are separate hiring workstreams entirely.
- **Ongoing performance, conduct, or disciplinary matters** unrelated to background investigation findings. Those belong to a future Workforce Governance module.
- **The mechanics of how background checks are ordered, vendors used, or turnaround times.** This module assumes a completed background investigation report exists and governs what happens once it does.

### 2.3 Relationship to Other Governance

This is Module 1 of the Serve Workforce Governance Framework. It is intended to interoperate with future modules — onboarding, credentialing, disciplinary action — without duplicating their concerns. Where this module's boundary touches one of those future modules, that boundary is noted explicitly rather than resolved here.

## 3. Audience

- **Serve leadership and legal counsel** — as the approving authority for this module once it leaves Draft status.
- **HR and recruiting staff** — as the operators who execute the review workflow in [`05-review-workflow.md`](./05-review-workflow.md).
- **Future Serve OS engineering** — as the requirements source for the Background Eligibility Engine described in [`08-future-software-specification.md`](./08-future-software-specification.md).
- **Auditors and regulators**, in the event of inquiry — as the documented, dated record of how and why a classification was reached.

## 4. Status of This Document

This module is **Version 0.1 (Draft)**. It has not been reviewed by legal counsel and has not been adopted by Serve leadership. Nothing in this module — including the offense taxonomy, the classification boundaries, or the workflow — should be treated as binding organizational policy until that review is complete and its status changes accordingly. See [`decision-log.md`](./decision-log.md) for the record of this module's origin and any changes to its status.

## 5. Legal Basis

This module operates in an area governed by federal, state, and in some cases municipal law (including but not limited to fair-employment and background-screening statutes, ban-the-box and adverse-action requirements, and healthcare-specific exclusion and registry requirements). This module does not attempt to state what that law requires. Every point at which this framework touches a legal question is marked **"Requires Legal Review"** rather than asserted as a legal conclusion.
